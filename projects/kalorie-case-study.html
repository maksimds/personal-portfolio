Kalorie

Calorie tracking app prototype

Complete

I designed Kalorie, an iOS app for tracking calories and macros, built around the main reason people give up on tracking apps: logging food becomes a chore. Kalorie keeps input effort to a minimum and makes daily progress readable at a glance.

Problem: Death by a thousand entries

Plenty of people start tracking what they eat, and many quit soon after. The reason is rarely motivation alone: every meal means searching, guessing portions and doing math, three or more times a day. [Add a sourced statistic on tracking app drop-off, with a link.]

That friction is a design problem. If logging a meal took seconds and the payoff was instantly visible, people would keep going.

Research

Before designing, I looked at how people use existing calorie trackers and where they drop off. [Add what you actually did: apps you reviewed, people you talked to, reviews you read.]

  • Logging takes too longSearching a database and entering portions for every food turns a meal into a data-entry task.
  • Portions are guessworkPeople think in cups and pieces, not grams, so they either guess or give up on accuracy.
  • Numbers without meaningDense screens full of figures tell people what they ate, but not how their day is going or whether they are on track.

Solution: Less typing, more knowing

Kalorie is built on two ideas: make every log as close to one tap as possible, and turn the numbers into something you can read in a second. Five core screens (Home, Food Log, Add Food, History and Profile) cover the full daily loop, from logging a meal to seeing the week’s progress.

  • One-tap logging from Recent foods
  • Serving sizes that do the math for you
  • Calories left, visible at a glance
  • Streaks and weekly progress

Logging in a few taps

Most meals are repeats, so the Add Food screen puts Recent foods up front, each with its calories and a one-tap add button. New foods are a search or a barcode scan away, and category chips (Protein, Grains, Veggies, Fruits, Dairy, Snacks) help when you don’t know the exact name. In the Food Log, every meal has its own add button and a floating Add Food button is always within thumb reach.

Portions without the math

People think in cups, not grams. On a food’s detail screen, serving sizes are pills (½ cup to 3 cups) plus a stepper, and every nutrition value recalculates instantly. Calories are marked as the primary metric, and the main button says exactly what will happen: Add to Log · 120 kcal, logged under Lunch.

Your day in one glance

The Home screen answers one question first: how am I doing today? A large ring shows calories against the daily goal, with the amount left underneath. Below it, protein, carbs and fat each get a color-coded progress bar, followed by quick stats for calories burned, water, net calories and streak.

Progress that keeps you going

The History screen turns a week of logging into a story. A streak card rewards consistency (with a personal best to beat), and a bar chart compares each day to a goal line: today in lime, days over goal in orange. Weekly average, days on goal and best day sit underneath, followed by a day-by-day breakdown.

Built on a system

The whole file runs on Figma variables, components with variants and auto layout. Color and typography are token-driven, so every screen reflows correctly across content lengths and iOS device sizes. The dark, high-contrast interface uses one lime accent for progress and actions, and keeps calorie, macro and progress figures as the main job of every screen.

Impact: From idea to a clickable product in a month

In one month I took Kalorie from a problem statement to a complete iOS product: every core screen designed and wired into a fully navigable prototype, not a set of static mockups. The design-system foundation means new screens, a light mode or a rebrand can be added without redrawing anything.

Next, I would test the prototype with people who have quit tracking apps before, measuring how long it takes to log a typical meal and whether they understand their day from the Home screen in under five seconds. [Add any feedback you received, for example reactions to your LinkedIn post.]
