dōTERRA Russia Platform Redesign

A global-standard experience for Russian members, without losing what they rely on

In development

I lead the design of the dōTERRA Russia web platform redesign: a near-complete rebuild of the site in Figma with a new visual language aligned with dōTERRA’s global brand, while keeping the Russian catalog, structure and localized content that members depend on.

Problem: A platform that made simple tasks feel hard

The Russian site had grown dense, dated and visually disconnected from dōTERRA’s global brand. Members struggled with the things they do most: finding products, signing up and placing orders. Help was limited to two small links, a returns policy and a PDF about the loyalty program. [Add a real number if you have one, such as the share of sign-ups abandoned.]

We saw a clear opportunity: give Russian members the same polished experience as the rest of the world, without moving anything they had learned to rely on.

Research

To find out where people really got stuck, I interviewed regional stakeholders and dōTERRA customers, audited the existing site page by page, and compared it with dōTERRA’s US platform. [Add how many people you spoke with.]

  • Lost in a crowded layoutKey pages and actions were buried in dense, text-heavy screens with no clear hierarchy.
  • Sign-up felt endlessBecoming a member meant a long process with no sense of where you were or what came next.
  • Two audiences, one siteCustomers come to shop, advocates come to run their business. The Russian site put business tools first and found products by function (digestion, metabolism, relaxation), unlike the brand-led US site, so both had to stay.

Solution: American look, Russian structure

The redesign keeps everything Russian members know (navigation labels, product categories, business tools and all localized content) and replaces everything they see: visual language, components, spacing, typography and calls to action. Members get a modern, on-brand platform that still feels familiar on day one.

  • Step-by-step sign-up with clear progress
  • Order summary always in view
  • Familiar categories, cleaner navigation
  • One design system behind every page

A sign-up that shows you the way

Becoming a member used to feel like one long form. I broke it into six short steps: choose a starter kit, review the order, create an account, add delivery details, pay, and confirm. A progress indicator sits at the top of every screen, so people always know where they are and how much is left.

No surprises at checkout

Checkout uses a two-column layout: forms on the left, the order summary always visible on the right. Members can see exactly what they are paying for at every moment, which removes the second-guessing that leads to abandoned orders.

Familiar structure, cleaner surface

Russian members find supplements by what they do, not by brand line, so I kept the functional categories and rebuilt the mega menu around them in clean card columns. Advocates’ business tools stay in a top utility bar, slimmed down so they no longer compete with shopping. A full-width lifestyle hero with a single call to action replaced the crowded first screen.

One system behind every screen

To keep a platform this large consistent, I am building a design system in Figma: color, typography (Raleway), spacing and radii as variables; buttons, form fields, cards, a progress stepper and an order summary as components with variants; and page layouts built with auto layout so they adapt across screen sizes. A brand change now happens in one place instead of hundreds.

Impact: A platform ready to grow

Almost the entire dōTERRA Russia platform is now redesigned and handed off to engineering with annotated specs, interaction states and responsive layouts. Along the way I set the design direction and led recurring reviews with business stakeholders and engineering, presenting and defending each decision.

Once the new platform launches, success will be measured by sign-up completion, checkout drop-off by step, time to first order, and support requests about ordering. [Replace with real results after launch.]
